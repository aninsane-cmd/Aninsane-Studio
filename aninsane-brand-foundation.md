# Aninsane — Brand Foundation

*Layer 1 (Positioning) & Layer 2 (Differentiators)*
*Revised: pivot to rig-ready OC/VTuber model art as the long-term flagship, illustration as entry point/side work*

---

## Layer 1: Positioning Statement

> Aninsane creates rig-ready OC and VTuber model art — characters marked by divine or mythical origin, illustrated and structured so a rigger can bring them into Live2D — for creators who want a character built to move, not just built to look good. Illustration commissions stay open as an entry point and a way to experience the style before commissioning a full model.

**Who it's for:** OC owners and VTubers who want a model made, but are hiring (or plan to hire) a separate rigger — so the job is to hand over art that doesn't create extra work for whoever rigs it. Illustration clients remain a secondary audience: people who may convert into model clients later.

**Why it matters to them:** a lot of rig-ready art is either poorly separated (riggers have to redraw parts) or visually flattened into something generic. Aninsane aims to solve both — real separation *and* a signature look that survives it.

**Reason to believe:** not yet established — see caveats below. This is the current gap between the claim and the proof.

## Layer 2: Differentiator Stack

| Differentiator | Status | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Hair rendering** | ✅ Proven (illustration) / 🔶 Untested (rig-ready) | Soft, ambient color-shift within a narrow hue range, no direct lighting on hair — the core signature, proven across illustration work. Not yet tested under strand-separation for rigging; splitting hair into movable layers risks introducing hard edges that work against the soft, continuous shift. |
| **Asymmetric rendering (flat where needed, painterly where it counts)** | New / Unproven | Face, body, and clothing shaded flatter and more separable to serve the rig, while hair keeps the atmospheric treatment. | **Thematic lane (divine/mythical marks)** | 🔶 Emerging, now technically reinforced | Characters marked by divine or mythical origin — dragon-blood, angelic/demonic nature, goddess-touched. These marks (horns, charms, floating ornaments) double as natural, self-contained rig parts, so the theme and the rig-ready structure reinforce each other rather than being separate claims. |
| **Rig-ready structure itself** | ❌ Not yet demonstrated | Clean part separation, sensible layering, sufficient hidden coverage for deformation. This is the actual technical gap — nothing in the current portfolio proves this yet. |

Kept as an illustration-side differentiator that builds trust and audience; largely not applicable to the rigged-model deliverable itself, which is typically delivered on a clean/transparent background. |

## Honest caveats (don't skip these)

- **The core untested assumption: does the hair signature survive separation?** The whole differentiator stack leans on "soft ambient hair rendering + rig-ready structure" being compatible. That hasn't been tested. Splitting hair into strand-movable chunks for rigging can force harder edges between pieces — which would undercut exactly what makes the rendering feel delicate. This should be tested on one piece before building a portfolio or pricing model around the claim.
- **Rig-ready conventions aren't self-taught from illustration instinct.** Layer separation standards, how much hidden coverage to draw under moving parts, where hard edges are unavoidable — this is worth a short conversation with an actual rigger, or a Live2D prep guide, before committing to a portfolio direction. Guessing at "rig-ready" without that input risks producing art that's rig-*styled* but not actually usable.
- **Format and thematic lane still carry over from the illustration-first plan, but their job has changed.** They're no longer proving "this is a strong illustrator" on their own — they're proving "this artist understands how a mythical mark functions as both story and structure." That's a more specific, harder claim, and it isn't demonstrated yet.
- **Rigging-it-yourself is explicitly deferred, not abandoned.** Worth revisiting as a long-term expansion once rig-ready art is a proven, repeatable service — but it should not creep into near-term goals or portfolio planning.

---

## What's next

The highest-leverage move now isn't more illustration practice — it's a small, focused test: take one existing or new piece, rebuild it (or a section of it, like the hair) with proper rig-ready layer separation, and see whether the soft color-shift holds up. That single test answers the one open question the whole positioning depends on, before more portfolio pieces get built on an unverified assumption.

Once that's resolved, the next real deliverable is a rig-ready OC or VTuber model piece — start to finish — as the first genuine proof point for "rig-ready structure" and "asymmetric rendering," the two differentiators currently sitting at zero.

Layer 3 (personality/voice) stays parked until there's a stronger evidence base under Layer 1 and 2, same as before.
